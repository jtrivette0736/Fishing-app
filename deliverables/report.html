1. What the app is

Name: Bench Tag. Tagline: Every reel on the bench, every customer in the loop. (docs/product-definition.md §1; three names were checked, benchtag.com is registered and parked, S725 / PD13.)

Pitch. Bench Tag is job tracking for the one-person reel and rod shop. A customer sends in a reel or orders a rod from the shop's own public page and pays the deposit; the shop sees every job on one board with a promised date; parts are quoted and approved online instead of by phone; the customer watches a private status page and gets an email at each step; the shop sends a payment link when the work is done and the money lands in the shop's own Stripe account. It costs $1 per job, never more than $29 a month, and $0 in a month the shop is closed (PD §1b).

Target user (the buyer). A reel technician, rod repairer or custom rod builder: one person, often part-time and seasonal, doing 10–100 jobs a month (S485 / V3: "fixing reels at a clip of about 100 a month"; S489 / V11: "backed up 35 orders"; S487 / V5: "keeping me busy on a part time basis"), taking 50% deposits and parts payments by hand (S491 / V13; S496 / V22), with intake by text, call, printed form or a two-hour meeting (S484 / V2; S486 / V4; S551 / V47; S489 / V11). What they will not do: learn a POS, pay $99 a month, hold a customer's money, or scale (PD §2a; S494 / V20; S543 / N-V17). Customer zero is the founder's own workshop, Storm Proof Tackle in Virginia Beach, whose site today has a dead "Book NOW" button and a "$0.00" repairs heading (S340 / P1; S672 / R21).

The end user (the shop's customer). An angler who left a $30–$500 reel with a shop and wants to know when it comes back, who found the shop through a forum or Facebook (S552 / V51; S140 / M20) and will switch shops over silence (S547 / V40; S548 / V44). The product has to deliver the positive version of the same need: "my reels are always done before the estimated date given at drop off" (S547 / V41).

Not the target in v1 (PD §2c): multi-tech shops with counters and staff (served by RepairDesk and Tower at $99–A$298, S698 / GA5, S524 / VS42); anyone whose problem is point-of-sale or parts stock; directories and marketplaces; OEM service centres; anglers as paying users.

2. The problem it solves

2a. What they do today

Intake arrives by phone, text, Facebook message, a printed form shipped in the box, a web form that is re-typed into a text, or a two-hour meeting; the queue is dates written on the outside of the box or a list in the tech's head; status is a phone call from the customer, or from the tech when the work is done; money is a 50% deposit collected by hand (builders), parts prepaid through a hand-raised invoice, cheques that must clear, or cash on pickup; uncollected work is handled by a rule printed on a paper receipt (research/evidence-repair-shop-tool.md §1 summary). Three of the seven shops with public price lists close for 4–8 weeks in season (S408 / P69; S409 / P70; S410 / P71), and the one mainstream tool a rod builder tried, Square, cannot express a two-to-three-week lead time (S483 / V1).

Row-level security in plain English (PD §7c). Every table has RLS on. A signed-in person can read and write only rows whose shop_id belongs to a shop they are a member of; status, token hash, job number and billing columns change only through SQL functions, never by a direct update; history and usage rows cannot be edited or deleted by anyone but the server. Anonymous visitors have no table access at all: they reach the public page through one function (public_shop(slug)), the status page through another (job_by_token), and may decide a quote through a third (decide_quote_by_token). Edge functions and cron use the service role. The test file must prove, for two shops A and B, that A cannot read or write any B row in any table, that a wrong token returns nothing, and that a quote that is not sent cannot be decided; the code-review skill blocks a merge without it.

The token approach (PD §7b). Each job's status link carries 16 random bytes (128 bits) encoded as 22 characters; only its SHA-256 is stored, so a leaked table or backup exposes no live links. "Resend link" rotates the token. The page shows the shop, the item, the status, the promised date, customer-visible history and photos, the current quote and the balance; never internal notes, other jobs, customer PII beyond first name, or Stripe ids. Lookups with no match are counted in rate_limits and reported in the daily digest. The red team added the short code + QR so the page is reachable from the printed receipt when an email goes to spam (change 2).

Storage buckets (PD §7d): job-photos (private, per-shop folders), job-photos-shared (public by exact path, for photos the shop marks customer-visible), shop-assets (logo, hero). Photos are resized on the device to ≤300 KB before upload (S702 / GA9).

7e. Break-even

Fixed costs ÷ net per shop: $46 ÷ ($19 − $0.98) = 3 shops; all-part-timer case $46 ÷ ($10 − $0.66) = 5 shops (S705 / GA12).
