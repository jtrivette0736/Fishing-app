# Market sizing: US recreational fishing (and the world, briefly)

Prepared 2026-10-20 by the Market Sizing Agent. Every number carries a source id (M-series,
table at the end). Populations are stated each time because the three big sources count
different people: RBFF/Outdoor Foundation counts Americans **6+** who fished at least once;
USFWS counts residents **16+**; license data counts **paid license holders** (many anglers are
exempt or unlicensed). Never add across them. Where a figure is derived, the math is shown and
the row says `estimate`.

Tooling note: the shared web-search budget ran out part-way through; later work used direct
fetches of primary PDFs. Google Trends could not be pulled, so seasonality rests on state
license-sales and tournament data, not search interest.

## 1. Summary (quotable)

1. **57.0 million Americans aged 6+ fished in 2025 (18% of the population), the first
   year-over-year drop since 2021**, down from the 57.9M record in 2024 but 14% above 2019's
   50.1M (M1, M2, M3).
2. **The bucket leaks a third of itself every year:** in 2025, 18M joined or returned (6.3M
   first-timers, 11.7M returning) and 19M quit; the churn rate was 33%, the highest on record
   (M4). USFWS's stricter three-year definition puts angler retention at 50% (M32).
3. **USFWS 2022 (16+): 39.9M anglers, 785M days, 463M trips, $99.4B spent, i.e. $2,490 per
   angler**, of which $917 is trip spend and only **$217 is fishing tackle** (M27, M28).
4. **Paid license holders: ~32.2M (2023) with gross license revenue of $841M for 2023 sales
   and $871M for 2024 sales** (~$26–27 per holder per year, E1); the top 10 states hold ~44% of
   holders, led by Texas (1.84M), California, Florida, Minnesota and North Carolina (M38, M39,
   M40).
5. **Freshwater is 43M (6+), saltwater a record 15.5M, fly 7.9M** (M5–M7); USFWS puts them at
   35.1M / 12.7M for 16+ (M27). Saltwater anglers took 204M trips in 2023, 61% from shore (M42,
   M43).
6. **Bass is the biggest species segment but has no current federal count:** the last USFWS
   species addendum found 10.7M black bass anglers = 38% of freshwater anglers (2001); applied
   to 2022 that is ~12–15M (M33, estimate E3). Organized tournament anglers are far fewer:
   roughly 0.3–1.0M a year (E5).
7. **Digital spend is tiny:** anglers spent $166M on *all* online media subscriptions in 2022,
   about $4 per angler per year; Fishbrain, the category leader, reported ~$15M revenue in 2023
   against a claimed 20M registered "anglers" (<$1 per user) (M35, M60, M62).
8. **Only ~25% of active anglers used a fishing app in the last Southwick survey (c. 2018)**,
   and 46% of non-users said they want to "leave technology behind" (M59); yet "local information
   on nearby bodies of water" is the #1 thing anglers say would help them, and "comprehensive
   mobile guides" ranks #4 (M17).
9. **The crowd is older, male, modest-income and mostly casual:** USFWS 16+ anglers are 67%
   male, 49% aged 45+, median household income ~$55k; 68% of all participants fish fewer than 12
   times a year and only 32% fish monthly or more (M29, M13).
10. **Adjacent B2B pools are small and fragmented:** ~15,500 tackle shops (IBISWorld, incl.
    online), ~6,900 saltwater for-hire vessels on the Atlantic/Gulf alone, ~5,700 charter
    enterprises by IBISWorld's narrow definition, >2,000 B.A.S.S. Nation clubs, and on the
    order of 50–90k fishing tournaments a year (M44–M47, M52, M55, E8).

### The 2020–21 spike and its retention

- RBFF: 50.1M (2019) → 54.7M (2020) → 52.4M (2021) → 54.5M (2022). Roughly half of the 2020
  gain was lost in 2021 and recovered by 2022; by 2025 participation was 6.9M (+14%) above 2019
  (M3).
- Outdoor Industry Association's own panel split (same SMS/PAC survey) shows the same shape for
  freshwater: 39.2M (2019) → 42.6M (2020) → 40.9M (2021); saltwater 13.2M → 14.5M → 13.8M; fly
  7.0M → 7.8M → 7.5M (M85).
- Licenses, Minnesota (all angling licence types, license years, M69): 963k (2019) → 1,059k
  (2020, +10%) → 1,009k (2021) → 940k (2022) → 914k (2023, *below* 2019) → 970k (2024) → 974k
  (2025). Early-season 2020 sales were up 45–48% on 2019 (M70, M71), so the spike was
  front-loaded and most of it churned out within three years; 2024–25 sits ~1% above 2019.
- England rod licences: 943,760 (2021/22) → 903,216 (2022/23, lowest this century) → 934,000
  (2024/25) (M75, M76). Same pattern: a post-COVID trough, then partial recovery.

metro areas, and 74% for motorized boaters vs 42% for non-boaters (M32). Boat ownership is the
strongest retention marker in the federal data.

## 3. Licenses

**National.** The TRACS dashboard reports total gross cost of fishing licenses of
$871,323,098 for the 2026 apportionment year, which (two-year lag) is **2024 licence sales**,
up 3.6% from $841,062,659 (2023 sales) (M38). Statista's copy of the USFWS series shows
**32.24M paid holders in 2023** and 30.26M in 2022 (M39; the page is paywalled and the figures
come from its public snippet, so treat ±0.5M). Implied gross cost per holder ≈ $841M / 32.2M =
**$26 per holder per year** (estimate E1). Licence fees are a small slice of angler spend: USFWS
2022 counts $1.64B of licence, combination-licence and stamp spending (M35) against $99.4B total.

RBFF adds that 53% of licences are bought in a store, 30% online, 15% at a licence office, and
**9% of participants fished without a licence** in 2025 (down from 11%); 43% of those said they
fished private waters and 31% did not know they needed one (M16).

Notes on the estimates:

- **E5, tournament anglers.** Driscoll et al. counted 41,939 black-bass tournaments a year in 14
  southeastern states (2009–11) and cite Schramm & Hunt (2007) that the Southeast held 51% of
  US competitive events in 2005; >80% of US tournaments target black bass (M55). If that share
  held, the US total was ~82,000/yr around 2010; recent state registers show Connecticut 633
  (2025, M56) and West Virginia 752 (2021, M57). Working range: **50–90k tournaments/yr** (E8).
  Connecticut's average field is 13.41 anglers (M56) → 0.7–1.2M angler-entries; at 1.2–3 events
  per competitor per year → 0.3–1.0M unique tournament anglers (E5). Low confidence; no
  national registry exists.
- **E10, charter customers.** Only the Northeast has a passenger count (1.6M in 2011, ME–NC,
  M45). The Southeast/Gulf fleet is similar in vessel count (~3,000, M44, vs 3,876 in the NE,
  M45) and the Gulf and Florida take the most trips (M43), so 2–2.5× the NE figure plus
  Pacific/Alaska/Hawaii gives 4–7M angler-trips; unique customers assume 1.5 trips each.
  Freshwater guide clients are extra and uncounted.

## 6. Demographics

**Gender.** 37% of participants 6+ are female (21.1M; up from 15.7M in 2015, +34%) (M9);
USFWS 16+: 26.9M male, 12.5M female (31%), 0.4M other (M29). Women are 41% of first-timers but
51% of those who quit (M21, M18); USFWS retention is 42% for women vs 55% for men (M32).

Sources: M11, M29. Among adults 16+, 19.5M (49%) are 45 or older. RBFF: "Nearly 4 in 10
participants were 45 or older" (M11). More than half of lost participants are over 45 (M18).

**Income.** RBFF 2025: ≤$25k 16%, $25–50k 21%, $50–75k 19%, $75–100k 13%, ≥$100k 31% (26% a
decade ago) (M11). USFWS 2022: median angler household income ≈ $55,000; participation peaks at
20% for $150–200k households and is 11–15% below $35k (M29). Fly anglers are the most bimodal
(37% under $50k, 35% over $100k) (M7).

**Urban/rural.** USFWS: 70% of anglers live in urban areas (13% participation rate), 30% rural
(23% rate); metros of 1M+ hold 39% of anglers at the lowest rate (13%); new entrants are even
more urban (76/24) (M29, M32). RBFF regions: South 44% of anglers (>25M), Midwest 13M, West 10M,
Northeast 8M (M12).

**Growth groups.** Hispanic participants doubled from 3.4M (2015) to 6.9M (2025), 16% rate,
+11%/yr since 2022; Black participants ~6.0M (15% rate, highest since tracking began 2007);
Asian Americans have the highest USFWS participation rate (20%) (M10, M29). Fishing remains
whiter than the country: 73% White vs 57% of the population (M11). Youth 6–12 slipped to 7.4M,
13–17 rose to 5.4M (M8). First-time saltwater anglers were 1.2M (8% of saltwater anglers) (M22).

## 7. Seasonality

Google Trends was not retrievable in this session (see tooling note). The license and
tournament evidence:

- **Spring opener spike (freshwater north).** Minnesota had sold 217,100 fishing licences by
  the Friday a week before the 2019 opener (early May) against 1,100,256 for the full year
  (~20% of annual sales before the season even opens); in 2020 the same date was 320,523 (+48%)
  and 354,080 by 4 May (+45%) (M70, M71). North Dakota's resident sales were +37% at the same
  point (M70).
- **Peak month is May–June for tournament activity.** West Virginia bass-tournament hours
  peaked in May at 25,960 of 135,231 for the year (19%) (M57).
- **Winter trough with a small ice bump.** Minnesota sold >56,000 licences between
  Thanksgiving and 13 January, roughly 5–6% of a ~1M year (M72); Explore Minnesota says about
  10% of the state's 1.5M licensed anglers ice fish (M73).
- **Trips are impulsive, not planned:** 36% of 2025 trips were unplanned and 46% planned less
  than a week out; only 5% were planned a month or more ahead (M15). A planning product sees
  demand concentrated in the days before a weekend, not months before.
- **Counter-seasonal segments.** Saltwater is concentrated in the South (56% of saltwater
  anglers, M6); West Florida alone generated 40.3M trips and East Florida 35.3M in 2022, and the
  Gulf region 55.2M (M43): these fisheries run year-round, with Florida/Texas winter effort
  offsetting the northern freeze. Ice fishing (Dec–Mar) is the other counter-seasonal pocket,
  2–3.5M anglers (E9). Fly fishing has the lowest avidity (45% fish 1–3 times) and the most
  seasonal waters (M7).

Implication for a subscription: annual plans sold March–May, with retention tested every
January.

## 10. TAM / SAM / SOM scaffolding

Common inputs: price anchor $35/yr (onX Fish $34.99, M65; Fishbrain UK ≈ £30, M64); app
adoption 25% (M59, c. 2018) used as low, 30% base, 40% high; avidity filter "fish monthly or
more" = 32% of participants (M13); adults = participants minus youth (57.0M − 12.8M = 44.2M,
M1, M8). SOM capture rates are deliberately small for a solo founder: consumer 0.1% / 0.3% /
1.0% of SAM in years 1–3; B2B 0.5% / 1.5% / 3%. The lead should cap SOM at comparable paid bases
from the competitor file (C-series) once available; the only public anchor here is Fishbrain's
~$15M global revenue (M62).

### (a) US anglers who use a smartphone app for fishing

```
Adults who fish        = 57.0M (M1) − 12.8M youth (M8)             = 44.2M
App users (TAM, people)= 44.2M × 25% / 30% / 40% (M59)             = 11.1M / 13.3M / 17.7M
TAM ($)                = people × $35 (M65)                         = $387M / $464M / $619M
SAM                    = monthly+ adults 44.2M × 32% (M13) × app %  = 3.5M / 4.2M / 5.7M
SOM (yr 1–3)           = SAM × 0.1% / 0.3% / 1.0%                   = 3.5k / 13k / 57k subs
                                                                    = $0.12M / $0.45M / $2.0M ARR
```
Range driver: adoption share (the 25% figure is eight years old) and whether avid anglers are
reachable without a free tier.

### (b) Tournament / club bass anglers

```
Unique tournament anglers (E5)      = 0.3M / 0.5M / 1.0M
TAM ($) at $50/yr (tool, not media) = $15M / $25M / $50M
SAM = club-affiliated or app-using  = 40% → 0.12M / 0.20M / 0.40M   (B.A.S.S. Nation 25k is the sourced floor, M52)
SOM = SAM × 0.3% / 1% / 3%          = 360 / 2,000 / 12,000 subs = $18k / $100k / $600k ARR
```
Range driver: the tournament count (50–90k/yr) and events per competitor. B2B angle: 5–15k
clubs (E12) at $200/yr = $1–3M TAM.

### (c) Saltwater anglers who need tide / regulation information

```
Saltwater adults   ≈ 15.5M (M6) × (1 − 18% youth share, M22 age bands) ≈ 12.7M  (= USFWS 12.7M 16+, M27)
TAM ($)            = 12.7M × $35                                      = $445M
SAM                = 12.7M × app % 25/30/40%                           = 3.2M / 3.8M / 5.1M
SOM                = SAM × 0.1% / 0.3% / 1.0%                          = 3.2k / 11k / 51k = $0.11M / $0.40M / $1.8M ARR
```
Filter rationale: every saltwater trip needs tides and regs, so no avidity cut; 24% of
saltwater anglers fish monthly+ and 42% fish 1–3 times (M22). Free incumbents (NOAA tides,
state regs apps, Navionics) press the capture rate toward the low case.

### (d) Anglers who own 5+ reels (gear-maintenance market)

```
Avid anglers (monthly+)          = 57.0M × 32% (M13)                    = 18.2M; adults ≈ 80% → 14.6M
5+ reel owners                   = 14.6M × 40% / 55% / 70% (assumed)    = 5.8M / 8.0M / 10.2M
Spend context                    : rods & reels $2.3B/yr (M35); 79% of adults bought gear in 12 months (M14)
TAM ($) at $15/yr log/maintenance= $87M / $120M / $153M
SAM = app users 30%              = 1.7M / 2.4M / 3.1M
SOM = SAM × 0.1% / 0.3% / 1.0%   = 1.7k / 7k / 31k = $26k / $108k / $460k ARR
```
No source gives reel counts per angler; the ownership shares are assumptions and this is the
weakest segment estimate (E14). Repair spend is buried in USFWS "auxiliary equipment" ($4.3B,
M28) with no breakout.

### (e) Fishing guides / charters as B2B customers

```
Businesses (E11)               = 12k / 18k / 25k
Revenue pool                   = $2.2B guide spend (M35)
TAM ($) at $600/yr SaaS        = $7.2M / $10.8M / $15M
SAM = viable full-time ops     ≈ 50% (over half of NE charters lost money, M45) = 6k / 9k / 12.5k
SOM = SAM × 0.5% / 1.5% / 3%   = 30 / 135 / 375 customers = $18k / $81k / $225k ARR
```
Range driver: the uncounted inland-guide population; willingness to pay is capped by thin
margins (M45).

### (f) Kayak anglers

```
Kayak anglers 6+ (E6)   = 4.0M (2025); 2.3M (2015) → ~5.7%/yr growth
Adults ≈ 80%            = 3.2M
TAM ($) = 3.2M × $35    = $112M
SAM = app % 25/30/40%   = 0.8M / 0.96M / 1.3M
SOM = × 0.1/0.3/1.0%    = 0.8k / 2.9k / 13k = $28k / $100k / $450k ARR
```
Range driver: the 7% "fished from a kayak" share counts anyone who did it once; dedicated kayak
anglers are fewer.

**Reading across:** at a solo founder's realistic capture, every consumer segment lands in the
$0.1–0.5M ARR base case; the differences are in competition and willingness to pay, not in
TAM. B2B (guides) is smaller still unless pricing is per-booking rather than per-seat.

## 11. Why this market is harder than it looks

1. **Free, ad- and hardware-funded incumbents.** Fishbrain runs a free tier with a claimed 20M
   base and still only reaches ~$15M revenue with losses (M60, M62); Navionics is bundled into
   Garmin's ecosystem and already claimed 1.5M app users (M67); state agencies and NOAA give
   regulations and tides away. The most-used apps in the Southwick survey were a free solunar
   app, Navionics and Fishbrain (M59).
2. **Low digital ARPU.** $166M of online media subscriptions across 39.9M anglers is $4 per
   head per year (M35), versus $917 on trips and $695 on boats and vehicles (M28). Anglers
   spend on going, not on software.
3. **Most anglers are casual.** 68% fish fewer than 12 times a year, 32% fish 1–3 times, and
   weekly anglers fell from 8% to under 6% in a decade (M13). Fly anglers: 45% fish 1–3 times
   (M7). A subscription has few days a year to prove itself.
4. **Churn is structural.** 19M people left in 2025 and only 39M of 57M were continuing
   participants (M4); USFWS three-year retention is 50% (M32). A quarter to a third of any
   acquired base disappears from the hobby each year before product churn is counted.
5. **Seasonality.** Roughly a fifth of Minnesota's annual licences sell before the season
   opens and winter sales are ~5% (M70–M72); West Virginia tournament effort peaks in May
   (M57). Northern-freshwater products face a 4–5 month dead zone; only Florida/Gulf saltwater
   (M43) and ice fishing (E9) run against it.
6. **Older, modest-income, less digital.** 49% of adult anglers are 45+ and the 65+ cohort is
   the fastest growing (M29, M11); median household income is ~$55k (M29); 46% of app non-users
   want to "leave technology behind" and 56% see no need (M59). 2025 saw the first participation
   decline since 2021 (M1).
7. **Trips are spontaneous.** 36% unplanned, 82% planned less than a week out (M15), and
   friends and family (58%, 49%) beat fishing websites (29%) as information sources (M15).
   Planning tools fight habit, not just competitors.
8. **Cost sensitivity.** "Too expensive" is the #3 reason for quitting and gear/licence cost is
   a named barrier (M17); 9% skip the licence altogether (M16).
9. **Data quality.** The federal survey changed method in 2022 and forbids trend comparison
   (M31); there is no current national count for bass, tournament, ice, surf or charter
   anglers, so segment sizes above carry ±30–50% error (E3, E5, E7, E9, E10).
10. **Thin B2B margins.** The average Northeast charter boat netted $5.1k in 2010 and over
    half lost money (M45); tackle retail is fragmented with no firm above 5% share (M47).
